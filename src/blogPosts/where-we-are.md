# The Journey to Glitch: From Hesitation to Creation

Hey everyone, it’s Entropy here. I want to share the story behind the creation of Glitch, a journey that began long before its actual development. Initially, Glitch was just an idea that we dreamed of bringing to life. Back then, I spent countless hours in Photoshop every day, crafting cool designs for our shirts and posters. To be honest, I was hesitant about the concept, and in many ways, I still am. I had never before created a product intended for thousands of users.

For most of my life, I made things for personal use or for the sheer joy of building something. I loved doing that. But deep down, I knew there would come a time when I needed to step up and create something that could earn money and bring fame. The task was overwhelming. However, at that stage, I was just a designer. The pressures of backend development, marketing, creating a good-looking front end, and handling the initial investment were Aditya's headaches, not mine.

Then, I received a call that changed everything. Aditya had tried to build the product by hiring random freelancers from the internet, paying them exorbitant amounts of money. Unfortunately, it didn’t work out. Every minor change or improvement came with a hefty price tag, and we simply didn’t have the funds to sustain that approach. We needed in-house development. And guess who could take on the monster that Aditya couldn’t slay? Me.

I took on the challenge of designing the front end using React. The journey was anything but smooth. At first, I dove straight into React tutorials, only to realize that I needed a more solid foundation. So, I went back to basics, learning HTML, CSS, and JavaScript. I even created a cool extension with a backend using Firestore. This learning curve pulled me into the complex and ever-evolving world of front-end development.

Front-end development is a vast field with numerous frameworks and methodologies. You could go the traditional route with PHP or JSP combined with HTML and vanilla CSS or Bootstrap. Alternatively, you could embrace the cutting-edge approach, Silicon Valley style, using React with TypeScript and Next.js as a framework. Either way, it was going to be a lot of work, but I was excited about the challenge.

Six months later, here I am, feeling ready to build the website. This doesn’t mean I know everything or that I am the greatest programmer (yet). But I believe we have waited long enough, and it’s time to make Glitch a reality.

### The Early Days: Conceptualizing Glitch

In the early days, Glitch was nothing more than a series of discussions and sketches. We brainstormed ideas, debated features, and imagined what our product could become. I spent hours each day in Photoshop, not just creating designs but trying to capture the essence of what Glitch could be. Each design was a step closer to making our vision tangible.

### Overcoming Doubts and Embracing the Challenge

Despite my excitement, I was filled with doubts. Could I really create something that thousands would use? I had always worked on personal projects, things that interested me or solved my own problems. This was different. This was about stepping out of my comfort zone and creating something for the masses. But deep down, I knew I had to take this leap.

### Taking Over the Development: A Turning Point

The call from Aditya was a turning point. His attempt to build Glitch by outsourcing the work had failed. It wasn’t just about the money; it was about control and understanding. We needed someone who could not only develop the product but also understand its core vision. That’s when I decided to take on the challenge. I knew it wouldn’t be easy, but I was ready to give it my all.

### Diving into Front-End Development

Starting with React tutorials was my first instinct, but I soon realized I needed a deeper understanding of the basics. I went back to learning HTML, CSS, and JavaScript. This foundational knowledge was crucial. I built small projects, each one a stepping stone toward the larger goal of creating Glitch. The more I learned, the more I realized how vast and varied front-end development could be.

### The Complexity of Choices: Frameworks and Tools

Front-end development offers a plethora of choices. You can go the traditional route with PHP, JSP, and vanilla CSS, or you can adopt modern frameworks like React, Angular, or Vue. After much consideration, I chose React with TypeScript and Next.js. This decision was not just about following trends but about choosing the right tools for the job. Each framework has its strengths and weaknesses, and making the right choice was critical for our success.

### The Path Ahead: Building Glitch

After six intense months of learning and development, I finally feel ready to build the website. This journey has been transformative. I’m not claiming to be an expert yet, but I’ve gained the confidence and skills needed to take Glitch from concept to reality. We’ve waited long enough, and it’s time to bring Glitch to the world.

This is just the beginning. There will be more challenges ahead, more learning, and more growth. But I’m excited to see where this journey takes us. Glitch is not just a product; it’s a testament to perseverance, learning, and the relentless pursuit of a dream. Stay tuned, because we’re just getting started.
